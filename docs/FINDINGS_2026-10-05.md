## 4. Sensor data stream — what the data actually is

- **App configures the sensors to 50 Hz** (`BleDeviceClient.targetRateRegister = RRATE_50HZ`,
  0x08), not 100.
- **The phone receives ~24.8 notifications/s per sensor at best** (`imu_diag.received`),
  i.e. exactly half. This is upstream of the app: received count == stored fresh frames, so
  the app's 10 ms tick loses nothing.
- **Delivered frames are 40 ms apart in sensor time, deterministically.** Measured by
  dividing the change in the sensor-integrated Euler angle by the gyro rate for consecutive
  received frames (restricted to high, near-constant rates): 77% of pairs at 35–45 ms, rest
  25–35 ms, essentially none at 10/20 ms. Same spacing whether the two frames reached the
  phone 10 ms or 50 ms apart. Under BLE stress (Saturday) a secondary bump at 80 ms = one
  dropped frame. Delivered frames are evenly spaced at 40 ms (~25 Hz), which is the BLE ceiling
  for this setup; the register value (50 Hz) is not what reaches the phone.
- **Where the halving happens is not provable from the data.** Likely one notification per
  connection interval (~40 ms) with the in-between frame overwritten in the sensor. The app
  cannot see the negotiated interval (hidden Android API), never requests a larger MTU, and
  does not log notification payload sizes. Cheap checks: log `bytes.size` once (rules out
  two frames per notification silently truncated by the parser, which reads only 20 bytes);
  run one session with the TIME packet enabled.
- **The 100 rows/s in InfluxDB are the recorder's 10 ms tick re-writing `latestSample`**
  with the tick's wall time (`IntervalRecordingService` sampling loop). ~75% of IMU rows
  are zero-order-hold repeats. Timestamps are tick times ≥ arrival times; the sensor's own
  clock (0x50 TIME frame) is never stored. The 0x61 frame has no timestamp or sequence number.
- "Pairs of fresh frames 10 ms apart" are an Android delivery artifact (two notifications
  handed over in one batch, drained one per tick); their true spacing is 40 ms.
- **Engine does not account for any of this**: `estimate_rate` sees 100 Hz; held rows stay in
  the signal (staircase); interpolation smears steps; catch precision is bounded by the
  ~80 ms burst cadence.

## 5. Analysis engine findings (`srow/analysis/`)

### 5.1 Feather-axis bug (affects every sculling interval)
PCA on raw gyro locks onto the **feather-flip axis** (500–850 °/s spikes once per stroke)
instead of the sweep. The stroke-band check cannot reject it because the flip also recurs
once per stroke. Confirmed: engine `sweep` axis is within a few degrees of the flip axis and
~90° from the true sweep on every oar sensor. Consequences: per-stroke match ρ ≈ 0.4,
most strokes flagged `low_confidence`, reported offsets (2 ms / 29 ms) not real.

Validated fix (ad hoc, not yet in repo): take PC1 of the **high-passed** (>2 Hz) gyro as the
flip axis, remove that component, then PCA the low-passed remainder → true sweep.
Pairing results on the main piece after the fix: Seat 1→Seat 3 ρ 0.94, lag +163 ms;
Seat 2↔Seat 3 ρ 0.95, lag −11 ms (warm-up piece). Physical sanity: drive arc 65–78°.

### 5.2 Other engine notes
- `analyze_async.py` (old research CLI) treats the hull sensor as a rower and its seat map
  sorts "Cox" before "Seat 1", shifting every seat label. Unusable for crews with a hull unit.
- No code anywhere fuses hull acceleration with GPS; boat speed is raw phone GPS only.
- Portal is batch-only; nothing real-time. Phone tier is causal, display-only.
- Portal `/interval` runs the full engine on click (~38 s for the 25-min piece).

### 5.3 Catch-timing accuracy — ranked ideas (not implemented)
1. **Recover the sensor clock from the frame cadence**: drop held rows, place delivered
   frames on a 40 ms grid anchored to arrival (lower envelope of arrival vs index), treat
   80/120 ms spacings as missing frames. ~80 ms quantization → ~10 ms. Needs no new data.
2. **Per-rower stroke template matching** (sub-sample x-corr against an averaged stroke).
3. **Fuse multiple markers per stroke** (catch reversal, finish reversal, square-up flip,
   feather flip, peak drive rate, oar accel entry spike), de-biased per rower.
4. **Smooth offsets across strokes** (random-walk smoother) for the "over 30 s" view.
5. **Use Seats 2+3 as the yardstick** after each step.

## 6. GPS findings

- ~4–12% of location rows per interval are **network-provider fixes** (accuracy 100–1300 m,
  no speed/bearing, 160–700 m off track). Cause in app: `requestLocationUpdates` is
  registered for **both GPS_PROVIDER and NETWORK_PROVIDER** with one listener; whichever
  fires last becomes `latestLocation`. Accuracy is stored but never used.
- **Timer re-writes**: the 10 ms tick re-adds `latestLocation` every pass; dedup only
  suppresses identical lat/lon within 1 s → stale fixes rewritten at 1 Hz. ~30% of rows
  are sub-second duplicates, ~30–35% identical positions.
- Stored timestamp is phone wall time at receipt, **not the fix's own time**.
- With re-writes removed, **real GPS holes** appear: up to 9 s in …355, 26–36 s in …353/354.
  In …353/354 the network runs cover genuine outages (nothing better existed); in …355 they
  were purely additive.
- A few timer rows mix a network position with the neighbouring GPS speed (fields written
  independently), so filter on accuracy, not on "speed present".
- App fixes: drop NETWORK_PROVIDER (or filter by provider/accuracy), keep `location.time`,
  write a row only on a new fix.

## 7. Phone app findings (`~/dev/SyncRow`)

- **TIME packet (0x50) is off** by default (`enableTimePacket=false`), and the connect
  sequence always writes RSW=0, switching it off even if saved in sensor flash. When on,
  the payload is counted and discarded.
- **BLE**: requests `CONNECTION_PRIORITY_HIGH` on connect and **re-asserts every 5 s on every
  sensor**, reads RSSI every 5 s, requests 2M PHY. Despite this, delivery is capped at
  ~25 notifications/s per sensor even with strong signal (the known BLE ceiling). Each re-assert triggers a
  connection-parameter renegotiation per link; if Android declines it, that is a procedure
  every 5 s for nothing and may itself cause short gaps.
- Parser handles exactly one 0x61 frame per notification (bytes 2–19); payload size not logged.
- Recorder: 10 ms tick (`targetSamplePeriodMs = 10`) stores `latestSample` per sensor with
  the tick's wall time; analyzer skips held ticks via a `fresh` flag.
- `sensorId` tag equals the seat label — no hardware identity in the data, so a chronically
  weak unit cannot be traced across sessions.

## 8. Hull sensor + GPS fusion — proposal (not started)
1. Calibrate hull frame from data: down = mean accel; forward = PC1 of horizontal accel
   (surge, stroke-band gated); sign resolved against GPS speed changes; sway = down × forward.
2. Gravity leakage is the crux (1° down error ≈ 0.17 m/s², same size/frequency as surge).
   Cancel with the sensor's fused roll/pitch per sample (test whether the on-chip filter
   absorbs surge), fallback gyro-integrated pitch.
3. Offline fixed-interval smoother: state = along-track speed + accel bias; measurements =
   GPS Doppler speed and inter-fix along-track distance, weighted by accuracy; estimate a
   per-interval fix-time lag; flag coasting across GPS holes > a few seconds.
4. Validate by holding out alternate GPS fixes; check surge minimum phase-locks to the catch.
5. Blockers today: hull sensor connected 4–19% on Saturday; needs link priority or its own
   phone; GPS holes of 9–36 s cannot be bridged by accelerometer integration.
